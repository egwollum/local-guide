Welcome to Arlington!

Arlington sits just across the Potomac River from Washington, D.C., and is one of the densest, most walkable cities in the country. Over the course of a week you can see a military cemetery, a national memorial, a riverside island, and some of the best food in the region. This guide is designed to help you plan your visit, without worrying about accessibility problem.

Top Tourist Spots

The Smithsonian

The Smithsonian Institution is a collection of 19 museums and the National Zoo, all free to enter. Located just across the Potomac in Washington, D.C., many are reachable by Metro from Arlington in under 20 minutes.

National 9/11 Pentagon Memorial

184 illuminated benches honoring the victims of 9/11. Open 24 hours.

Arlington National Cemetery

639-acre military cemetery holding 400,000+ veterans, the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, and the graves of Presidents Kennedy and Taft. Free.

Why Arlington?

You're a short ride from D.C.'s museums and monuments, but Arlington itself has plenty to fill your visit: Arlington Cemetery, the Pentagon Memorial, T. Roosevelt Island, and neighborhoods with their own character. The Metro means you can leave your car at the hotel, and most of the restaurants we recommend are within a few blocks of a station.
