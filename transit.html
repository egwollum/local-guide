Public Transit

Where to go, and how to get there.

The Washington Metro is one of the most accessible transit systems in the country, with tactile paving, audio announcements, and priority seating built into every station. This guide walks through how to navigate from street level to platform and back, so you can plan your route with confidence. Whether you're heading to the Smithsonian or a quiet bench at the Pentagon Memorial, getting there is easier than you might expect.

Navigating a DC Metro Station as a Visually Impaired Tourist

Before Your Trip

  • Visit the WMATA Website or call 202-GO-METRO (202-466-3876) to confirm your station's elevators are in service. Outages happen, and a free shuttle is offered when they do.
  • If you qualify for a Reduced Fare SmarTrip card (50% off), apply through the Transit Accessibility Center at 655 Virginia Ave SW, near L'Enfant Plaza station. Call 202-962-2700.
  • If you cannot use the rail system independently, you may qualify for MetroAccess, a door-to-door paratransit service. Visitors can apply for a 21-day pass within a rolling 365-day period.

  1. Find the station entrance

    • Look for the station pylon — the tall, colored column with the Metro "M" logo. It has the station name in Braille and raised lettering you can read by touch.
    • Directional signs with the International Symbol of Accessibility (wheelchair symbol) point to the accessible elevator entrance. Note that the elevator entrance may be at a different street corner than the main entrance.

  2. Enter the station

    • Use the accessible entrance marked with the wheelchair symbol. This leads to the elevator, not stairs or escalators.
    • If you're using a white cane, feel for the tactile paving (bumpy truncated domes) that marks the path to the elevator.

  3. Subway fare

    Fare can be purchased at each station. Self serviced machines typically line the wall on one side between the escalator and faregates. There should be an accessible machine available at all sations. If not, staff are present to provide assistance. Alternatively riders can pay directly with tap to pay using a mobile device.

  4. Pass through the faregate

    • Look for the extra-wide accessible faregate (wider than the standard gates). The SmarTrip reader is on both sides and on the top right.
    • Tap your card and wait for the positive tone — that confirms the gate is open.
    • If you need to buy a card, use the accessible fare vending machine, which has a lower panel, Braille/raised instructions, and a button that plays audio instructions.

  5. Down to the platform

    • Follow the directional signs with the wheelchair symbol and an arrow. They're on the platform information pylons and wall-mounted station name signs.
    • The PIDS (Passenger Information Display System) on the mezzanine will also announce elevator status.

  6. On the platform

    • Tactile paving (bumpy tiles) runs along the entire platform edge — your cane or feet will detect it. Stop before you reach it.
    • Flashing lights at the platform edge alert you when a train is approaching. You'll also hear the train's sound and feel the air pressure.
    • The PIDS display on the platform announces which line the next train is, its destination, and arrival time.
    • Emergency intercoms are on pylons marked with a white strip or the word "HELP." Instructions are in Braille and raised alphabet.

  7. Board the train

    • Trains announce the station name and line as they arrive and depart.
    • Gap reducers on every rail car close the gap between the platform and the car by an extra 1½ inches.
    • Barriers between rail cars are textured so you can feel the difference between a car door and the space between cars. Don't mistake the gap for a doorway.
    • Priority seating is at the center doors. The train operator will announce the direction of travel and upcoming stops.

  8. Exiting at your destination

    • Listen for the station announcement, or count stops (the operator announces each one).
    • When the train stops, wait for the doors to open and the chime.
    • Follow the tactile paving and directional signs back up to the mezzanine, then to the street-level elevator.

Tips for Visually Impaired Riders

  • All stations have tactile paving near platform edges.
  • Frequent audible announcements and updates are made on the platform and inside metro cars.
  • Call WMATA or use the WMATA app for help planning routes and checking elevator status.
  • Service animals and visual aids are welcome.
  • Staff members are available at all stations.
